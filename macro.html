Where the world is, by Bridgewater's compass.

Dalio reduces the macro problem to two questions. Is growth rising or falling, and is inflation rising or falling. The answers form four quadrants, and each quadrant has assets that historically outperform. Beneath that sits a longer arc: the rise and fall of credit and debt cycles. This page renders both, then asks how the existing concentrated thesis aligns or fights against them.

How this page is built
Macro indicators come from FRED when an API key is configured (free at fred.stlouisfed.org). Without a key, the page falls back to ETF-implied proxies — equity trend for growth, TIPS-vs-Treasuries spread for inflation, HY spreads for credit stress. The classification is rule-based and deliberately mechanical so it does not get talked into the wrong regime by recency bias.

Underweight: long-duration anything, levered equity

№ 03 The Long-Term Debt & Order Cycle

Dalio's longer arc holds that empires move through five phases — rise on sound money, debt and education; mature into productivity peaks; over-extend as reserve currencies weaken and internal disorder rises; and eventually undergo monetary reset. The United States, by his own framing, sits in late phase four — high debt, internal political disorder, contested reserve-currency status, rising external rival.

The implication for a concentrated portfolio is not that crisis is imminent. It is that the next cycle's winners are unlikely to be the same names, sectors, or even currencies as the last cycle's. Hard assets, productive capital outside the dominant power, and protection against monetary debasement deserve a structural sleeve regardless of regime.

№ 04 All-Weather Benchmark vs. Your Bet
Why this matters for a concentrated GLP-1 thesis
All-Weather is the canonical risk-parity portfolio — equal risk contribution across asset classes, not equal capital. Every concentrated thematic bet you take on the Five-Pillar or Watch ledgers should be measured against what you would have held in All-Weather instead. If your active bet does not measurably improve on the All-Weather expected return after considering the risk you are adding, the active bet is not earning its keep.

All-Weather template

The 40% long bond weight looks high to a tech-investor's eye. It is high on purpose — bonds are the regime hedge for the deflationary quadrant. The 15% in gold and commodities is the hedge for the stagflationary quadrant. Equities are the smallest single weight, despite being the highest expected return, because equities deliver that return most violently.

Your concentrated thesis (rotate from AI mega-cap to GLP-1) is a much more aggressive allocation. The All-Weather row is here as the diversified counter-example: every dollar you put into GLP-1 instead of into this template is a dollar betting that your thesis improves on regime-balanced returns. That bet may be defensible — but it deserves to be sized.
